# What's Changed

## New Features

### Every job a record went through, on the record page

Tag a job with the record it works on and give it a name:

```apex
Async.queueable(new ReserveStockJob(lines))
    .name('Reserve stock')
    .chainName('Order fulfilment pipeline')
    .linkRecord(order)
    .linkRecords(lines)
    .chain(new CommissionSplitJob(order)).name('Commission split').linkRecord(order).retry(2)
    .enqueue();
```

Drop the new `Async Jobs` component on the Order record page. One card per chain, newest first,
with status, timing and failure reason per step, finalizers under their parent, chunk runs as one
step with a page strip, and a chain map with the dependency arcs. The card opens the first
running chain, the others collapse to one line with a dot per step, or a progress bar when the steps do not fit. The card polls
only while something is in flight and never uses Platform Events, so it costs nothing from the
org's delivery allocation. A step that appears while you watch slides in with a New badge.

`linkRecord(...)` / `linkRecords(...)` take an `SObject`, an `Id`, a `List<SObject>`, a `List<Id>` or
a `Set<Id>`. `linkExternalRecord('NetSuite', 'Customer', '4711')` links a record that lives in
another system, by key, so that system can query what ran for it. Inside `work()`,
`Async.linkRecord(...)`, `linkRecords(...)` and `linkExternalRecord(...)` add the records a job
discovers, without DML. A linked job always writes its result row. The links are rows of the new
`AsyncResultLink__c`: `Key__c` indexed, `Source__c` (`Salesforce`, or yours), `ObjectName__c`,
unique per result and key, with a page layout, a compact layout and a related list on the
result. A replay reads its links back from these rows.
[Job Timeline](https://async.beyondthecloud.dev/features/job-timeline)

### See a chain while it runs

`TrackJobProgress__c = Yes` on `QueueableJobSetting__mdt` writes the result row at enqueue, as
`ENQUEUED`, and updates it in place when the job settles, with `StartedAt__c`, `FinishedAt__c` and,
while retrying, `NextAttemptAt__c` and the attempt count. Nothing is written inside `work()`, so
jobs that call out are not affected.

### Requeue failed steps in order, dependencies included

`Async.requeue(ids)` replays rows in their original chain order whatever order the ids arrive in.
`dependsOn` now travels with the row: a dependency on another row of the same call is rebuilt
between the replays, one on a row whose outcome already satisfied it is dropped and reported in the
new `RequeueSummary.droppedDependencyByResultId`, and one that was not met skips the dependent with
a reason. `Requeue failed steps` on the record page does this for a whole chain, behind the new
`AsyncRequeue` custom permission.

### `nextInChain()`

From inside a running job, place a new job right after the current one, before everything already
waiting and ahead of any priority:

```apex
public override void work() {
    Invoice__c invoice = createInvoice();
    Async.queueable(new CalculateTaxJob(invoice)).nextInChain().enqueue();
}
```

Finalizers of the current job still run first. Closes #68.

## Improvements

- `Async.JobContext` and `Async.FailureContext` carry `jobName` and `chainName`.
- A chain serialized before this version keeps running after the upgrade.
- An `ENQUEUED` row the cleanup batch removed while its job was delayed is written again when the
  job settles. A placeholder whose `System.enqueueJob` failed is deleted in the same transaction.
- The timeline keeps polling through a failed check and stops after three in a row, sleeps until
  the next scheduled retry when that is all that is in flight, watches for two minutes after a
  requeue, and flags an idle card that has not checked for ten minutes.
- The card has a range menu, counts and filters chains across the whole range, pages in memory
  through blocks of 50 chains, scrolls inside a configurable height, asks before a requeue, opens
  linked records and the replay of a requeued step.
- The chain map is a pipeline of nodes with elbow connectors for dependencies, opened on the
  first failed node.
- The card keeps a fixed height, fits its page size to it, refreshes in place, opens one chain by
  itself and flags newcomers, or follows them with `Follow new runs`. The legend opens as a panel
  over the list, so the card keeps its height. It carries the Async Lib logo and a footer naming
  the version.
- `ChainSequence__c` always follows run order. A job with a priority takes its real place in the
  chain, on the list, on the map and when requeued.
- A job added by a running job records who added it, whichever call added it.
- A delayed job shows as `Delayed`, with the time once it is enqueued and only its delay before that. The chain shows `DELAYED` before it starts and `RUNNING | DELAYED` after.
- Batch failures are handled 50 per delivery. A logger spending a DML per failure could hit the DML
  limit on a large delivery, and that rolled back every failure record in it.
- `AsyncResult__c` Recently Viewed, lookup and search results show status, names and timing.
- `AsyncResult__c` has fourteen new fields: `JobName__c`, `ChainName__c`, `ChainSequence__c`,
  `Priority__c`, `ParentCustomJobId__c`, `AddedByCustomJobId__c`, `ChunkRunId__c`,
  `ChunkPage__c`, `ChunkPages__c`, `DelayMinutes__c`, `Dependencies__c`, `StartedAt__c`, `FinishedAt__c`,
  `NextAttemptAt__c`; `Status__c` gains `ENQUEUED`. All on the layout and in `AsyncResultAccess`.
- `AsyncResult__c` ships with a tab, a Lightning record page, a compact layout and four list views:
  Failed (last 7 days), In flight, Replayable, By chain.
- New permission set `AsyncRequeueAccess` with the `AsyncRequeue` custom permission.
- The `AsyncResult__c` tab uses the gears icon.
- Internal: `AsyncResult__c` and `AsyncResultLink__c` rows are written by the new
  `AsyncResultWriter`, `QueueableJobSetting__mdt` is read through the new `QueueableJobSettings`;
  `QueueableChain` keeps only the chain logic. No API change.
- `QueueableJobSetting__mdt.MaxRecordLinks__c` caps links per job, 5000 by default and at most.

## Behaviour change to check

Bulk `Async.requeue` used to replay rows in whatever order the query returned them. It now follows
`ChainSequence__c`. Rows written before 3.2.0 have no sequence and keep the old, undefined order.

## Access

`AsyncResultAccess` is View All on `AsyncResult__c`, as in 3.1.0. With the record page card that
now means: everyone holding it sees every chain on every record, whatever their access to the
record. Give it only to people who may see all async results. Record-scoped access is tracked in
#77.
